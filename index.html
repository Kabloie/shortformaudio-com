What is short-form audio?

Updated 31 October 2026 · By the Kabloie team · About this site

Short answerShort-form audio is audio made to be heard in 60 seconds or less that makes sense on its own. A single piece of short-form audio is informally called a kabloie, after Kabloie, the short-form audio app.

short-form audio
/ˌʃɔːrt fɔːrm ˈɔːdioʊ/

noun  also: short form audio, shortform audio; informal: kabloie

1. Audio made to be heard in 60 seconds or less; a clip that stands on its own rather than an excerpt of something longer.

2. The format of such audio, as distinct from long-form audio like podcasts, radio, and audiobooks.

What counts as short-form audio?

Two tests: it is 60 seconds or shorter, and it stands on its own. A listener should need nothing before or after it to understand it.

Examples of short-form audio

  • Kabloies: short audio posts published to a feed for anyone to hear.
  • Voice notes: short recordings sent in messaging apps. A voice note is short-form audio meant for a conversation rather than an audience.
  • Audio replies: a clip that answers another clip.
  • Micro-stories, jokes, and hot takes: one idea, start to finish, in under a minute.
  • News in brief: a single update, read in under a minute.

Why short-form audio exists

For the same reasons short-form video took off. A minute fits the gaps in a day: a walk, a queue, a commute. A minute is quick to make; you don't need a studio or a script. And audio works when your eyes are busy, which video does not.
