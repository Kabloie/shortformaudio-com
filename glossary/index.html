The vocabulary of short-form audio, A to Z.

A

audiogram

noun

A video made from an audio clip, usually a still image with a moving waveform and captions, so the audio can be shared on video-first platforms.

C

clip

noun

A single, self-contained piece of audio. In short-form audio, the clip is the whole work, not a cut from something longer.

See: short-form audio

cold open

noun

Starting a clip mid-thought, with no introduction or title. The default in short-form audio.

See: hook

H

hook

noun

The first few seconds of a clip, which decide whether a listener stays.

See: Spec §4, Structure

K

kabloie

noun & verb · informal

A piece of short-form audio. As a verb: to record, publish, or send one.

Full entry: kabloie

L

long-form audio

noun

Audio meant for sustained listening, usually many minutes or hours long, such as podcasts, radio, and audiobooks.

Compare: short-form audio

LUFS

noun

Loudness Units relative to Full Scale: the standard measure of how loud audio sounds to a listener. Short-form audio targets −16 LUFS.

See: Spec §5, Sound

M

microcast

noun

A podcast whose episodes are only a few minutes long. An episode counts as short-form audio only if it is 60 seconds or less and stands on its own.

Compare: podcast

P

podcast

noun

A series of spoken-audio episodes distributed by feed. Usually long-form.

Compare: short-form audio

R

reply

noun

A clip that answers another clip.

See: Spec §7, replyTo

S

short-form audio

noun

Audio made to be heard in 60 seconds or less, complete on its own.

Full entry: short-form audio

soundbite

noun

A short excerpt from a longer recording, such as a speech or interview. Unlike short-form audio, a soundbite depends on its source.

T

transcript

noun

The text of everything said in a clip. The spec requires one for every clip with speech.

See: Spec §6, Accessibility

true peak

noun

The highest level a waveform reaches, including peaks between samples, measured in dBTP. Keeping it below −1 dBTP prevents distortion on playback.

V

voice note

noun

A short recording sent in a messaging app. A voice note is short-form audio meant for a conversation rather than an audience.

Compare: kabloie
